## Context

One product serves 4 kinds of users. Travellers browse more than 500 tours and book. Travel specialists plan trips with them. Partners run the tours on the ground and pick up work in AGORA, the partner portal, where they see new offers, place requests and track orders. Admins keep it all running. New features often went live on Triptile first, the group’s online trip builder, and moved to Firebird Tours later.

## What I did

- **Pages.** Reworked the main pages and journeys for each kind of user, including the tour catalogue and the traveller’s trip area.
- **Itinerary builder.** The most complex screen in the product. A traveller puts tickets, hotels and places into one plan.
- **AI assistant.** Designed the chat in which an AI assistant asks what the traveller wants and builds a trip plan from the answers.
- **Destination landings.** A page for each destination where a visitor picks how to start: talk to an expert, take a popular trip, build it themselves or describe it to the AI. The layout and its states are mine.
- **Event landings.** Pages for big events, like the Paris 2024 Olympics page with the games schedule, the regions and ready-made trips.
- **Research.** Called users and our travel specialists and went through their scenarios with them.
- **Team.** As design lead I split the work between 2 junior designers, reviewed it and handed it to development. Before major releases I ran design reviews.
- **Design system.** Owned this product’s components and patterns in the shared design system and presented them to analysts, managers, developers and designers.

## What research changed

The specialists’ screen was overloaded and dated. I made a minimal version and showed it to them on calls. It turned out they work with the whole trip at once, and opening dropdowns one by one cost them time. So the final version puts everything on one screen.
